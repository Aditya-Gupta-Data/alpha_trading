# Alpha Trading: Policy-Event Playbook

Oct 20, 2026 · @Aditya

## The way of thinking

You start from a policy headline and refuse to stop at it. Each question narrows from the deal, to the rule, to the exact products, to numbers, to who actually wins.

The path you took on the India-EU FTA, step by step:

1. **Get the primary facts first.** Status, dates, quota sizes, duty path. Not the headline "110% to 10%", the schedule behind it.
2. **Find the mechanism that touches prices.** Which cars qualify (imported or assembled here, where built, petrol or plug-in, above what price), and what the duty cut does to a showroom price after GST.
3. **Map who is exposed.** Which models are imports, which are local, which sit below the price floor. Then ask which Indian brands are really hit.
4. **Quantify over time, with ranges.** Base sales, how buyers respond to price, how many buyers come from rivals, a five-year path. No single-point answers.
5. **Pressure-test.** Is the quota binding? What else moves the same prices (the UK deal, local assembly plans)? What is excluded (plug-in hybrids)? Which inputs are estimates?
6. **Roll up to owners.** Car, then brand, then the listed parent (JLR rolls up to Tata).

Two steps turn this into a trade. The conversation pointed at them but did not run them:

7. **Materiality.** Is the gain big relative to the listed company, or a rounding error?
8. **What is already priced.** Compare the market's reaction on announcement day with what the mechanics justify. The gap is the signal.

The principles underneath:

- **Headline is not mechanics.** Markets react to the headline; money is made on the schedule's fine print.
- **Second-order beats first-order.** The obvious winner ("European luxury brands") is less useful than the specific one (a Slovakia-built SUV owned by an Indian company).
- **Route and origin decide exposure.** Same badge, different fate: an E-Class from EU kits gains, a GLE from US kits loses.
- **Numbers with ranges, sources and flags.** Every figure is either reported or an estimate, and says which.
- **Follow the money to a ticker.** A model-level effect matters only through a listed company.

## The idea: India-EU FTA

The FTA is a narrow luxury-import deal that the market traded as a mass-market threat. Its biggest single winner is Tata, through the Slovakia-built Land Rover Defender. The Indian mass-market makers that sold off were never exposed.

### The mechanics

| Item | What the text says |
| --- | --- |
| Status | Concluded 27 Jan 2026; signing expected Dec 2026; in force Feb to Mar 2027 |
| Car quota (petrol, diesel, mild hybrid) | 1 lakh in Year 1, 1.075 lakh in Year 2, 1.3 lakh in Year 5, 1.6 lakh from Year 10 |
| In-quota duty | 35% (EUR 15k to 35k) or 30% (above EUR 35k) in Year 1, down to 10% by Year 5; today 70% or 110% |
| Price floor | No cut below EUR 15k CIF; officials say cars likely to retail under ₹25 lakh will not be exported |
| EVs and plug-in hybrids | Nothing until Year 5, then a 20,000 quota at 30%, rising to 90,000 by Year 14 |
| Kits for local assembly | 75,000 kits a year, duty 16.5% to 13.75% in Year 1 and 8.25% from Year 3 |
| Today's EU imports | 17,191 cars in 2025, against a Year 1 quota of 1 lakh |
| Market reaction, 27 Jan 2026 | M&M down about 4 to 5% (about ₹18,000 Cr of market value), Maruti and Tata PV down 1 to 3% |

### What the model says (base case, five years combined)

- **Winners:** Defender +13,976 extra sales; Tiguan R-Line +1,862; Porsche Cayenne +1,499; AMG G63 +1,132.
- **Losers:** Mercedes GLE −1,513; BMW X5 −1,071; Mercedes GLS −809. All are assembled here from US kits, so they get no kit-duty relief.
- **By brand:** JLR +13,290; Toyota −628; Mercedes −575; BMW roughly flat.
- **Quota:** modelled EU imports use about 8% of it. Demand and pricing decide outcomes, not the cap.
- **No Tata- or Mahindra-badged model is touched.** The cheapest qualifying car lands above ₹30 lakh after duty and GST.

### What it means for a trade (hypotheses, not advice)

1. **The mass-market sell-off was a headline reaction.** M&M and Maruti sell where the deal cannot reach, and EVs are shielded for five years. Check whether those January moves have already reverted before acting.
2. **Tata's gain is real but small.** Year 5 extra Defender sales of about 3,763 units at about ₹74 lakh is roughly ₹2,800 Cr at showroom prices. That is under 1% of JLR's roughly £29bn yearly revenue (approximate, from memory), and less once GST and dealer margin come out. Not a standalone thesis.
3. **The losers are not listed in India.** Mercedes-Benz India, BMW India and Toyota Kirloskar are unlisted, so there is no direct short.
4. **Watch the catalysts:** signing (Dec 2026), entry into force (early 2027), monthly Vahan data for Defender against GLE, and JLR's decision on assembling the Defender locally.

The lesson the engine should learn: when the market reacts to a headline, measure the mechanics and the materiality before believing the move. I am not a financial advisor; treat these as research leads.

## Playbook the engine runs on any policy event

The engine runs ten steps on every policy or trade event and stops early when an event fails a gate. It fits trade deals, import duties, GST changes, PLI schemes, safeguard duties and RBI or SEBI rules.

1. **Detect and classify.** From the news feed, tag the event: type, issuer, stage (proposed, concluded, signed, in force) and key dates. Gate: skip pure rumours unless a primary source exists.
2. **Extract the mechanics.** Pull every number that changes: rates, quotas, thresholds, phase-in years, exclusions. Take them from the text or schedule, never the headline. Store each with its source link.
3. **Map exposure.** List the segments that qualify and those that do not, split by route (imported or made here), origin, price band and technology. Attach the listed NSE/BSE companies to each segment.
4. **Price transmission.** Rebuild the cost stack before and after (duty, cess, GST). Apply a pass-through range (low, base, high), since companies keep part of any saving.
5. **Demand response.** Apply a price-response range per segment. Split extra sales into buyers taken from rivals and new buyers. The taken share becomes the rivals' loss.
6. **Constraints and confounders.** Check whether caps bind (quotas, import allowances, capacity). List other policies moving the same prices and company plans that change the route.
7. **Materiality gate.** Convert the effect into revenue for each listed company and divide by its annual revenue. Below the threshold, record it but do not suggest a trade.
8. **Priced-in check.** Measure the stock's move from the day before to a few days after the announcement. Compare it with the material effect. A big move on an immaterial effect is an overreaction; a small move on a material one is an underreaction.
9. **Timeline.** Map when the effect reaches profits (phase-in years) against when news lands (signing, entry into force, monthly sales data, results). These dates become the watchlist.
10. **Suggest, never execute.** Output direction, horizon, entry logic, the condition that proves it wrong, and a confidence score. Send it to Discord for Approve or Dismiss, paper-trade it, and let plans resolve against real prices so the learning loop can reweight.

Steps 2, 3 and 7 carry most of the edge. Steps 4 and 5 make the numbers honest, and step 8 is where the trade comes from.

## Output format and guardrails

Every event produces one event card in this shape, filled step by step. A card that fails a gate is still saved, so the engine learns which events mattered.

```json
{
  "event_id": "india-eu-fta-2026",
  "type": "trade_deal",
  "stage": "concluded",
  "key_dates": {"concluded": "2026-01-27", "signing": "2026-12", "in_force": "2027-Q1"},
  "mechanics": [
    {"rule": "in-quota duty, CBU cars above EUR 35k", "before": 1.10, "after_y1": 0.30, "after_y5": 0.10, "source": "<url>"}
  ],
  "exposure": [
    {"segment": "EU-built luxury SUV imports", "direction": "positive", "listed": ["TMPV"], "route": "import"},
    {"segment": "mass-market cars under 25 lakh", "direction": "none", "listed": ["M&M", "MARUTI"], "route": "local"}
  ],
  "assumptions": {"pass_through": [0.45, 0.60, 0.75], "price_response": [-1.6, -2.0, -2.4]},
  "materiality": [{"ticker": "TMPV", "revenue_effect_pct": 0.8, "passes_gate": false}],
  "priced_in": [{"ticker": "M&M", "event_move_pct": -4.5, "justified_move_pct": 0.0, "gap": "overreaction"}],
  "catalysts": ["signing", "entry into force", "monthly Vahan data"],
  "suggestion": {"ticker": "M&M", "direction": "long", "horizon_days": 60, "invalidation": "<condition>", "confidence": 0.0},
  "flags": ["estimates used: Cayenne, G63 base volumes"]
}
```

Numbers in the example are illustrative placeholders, not live readings.

Guardrails:

- **Suggest only, never execute.** Every output ends in Approve or Dismiss.
- **Every number is tagged.** `reported` (with a source link) or `estimate` (with how it was made).
- **Ranges are mandatory.** No suggestion from a single-point estimate.
- **Materiality gate before any suggestion.** Default threshold: 2% of annual revenue, adjustable.
- **Headline check.** If the move came within a day of the headline and before the text was out, mark it a headline reaction.
- **Confidence starts low and is earned.** It rises only as resolved paper trades on similar event types come in.
- **No back-filled history.** There is no free historical news feed, so the engine cannot backtest events on its own. It logs live events from now on, plus a hand-built library of past events.

## Options for wiring this into alpha\_trading

Three ways in, from lightest to deepest. Each builds on the one before, so you can stop at any of them.

| Option | What gets built | Effort | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Prompt only | This doc saved as `docs/EVENT_PLAYBOOK.md`; the Gemini news step gets a prompt that runs steps 1 to 3, 7 and 8 per policy headline | Low | An "Event thesis" message in Discord next to normal alerts |
| B. Event module | `src/event_engine/` producing the event card; `config/exposure_map.yaml` (policy keyword to segments to NSE tickers); a materiality calculator; a priced-in check on yfinance prices around the event date | Medium | Cards with real numbers and gates, shown in the Phase 5 Discord bot |
| C. Learning loop | Option B plus suggestions paper-traded and resolved; a hand-built library of past events (this FTA, GST 2.0 in Sep 2025, the UK deal in Jul 2026, RBI MPC days) replayed on price data in Brain Map | Higher | Confidence per event type, earned from outcomes |

Option B's exposure map is the piece only you can seed well. It is the step-3 knowledge (which company sits in which segment and route) that the model cannot reliably invent.
